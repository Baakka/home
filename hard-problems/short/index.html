The Fibred Order of Difficulty

Difficulty is not stored inside a problem like mass inside an object. The same state can be recoverable through one sensor and invisible through another. The same target can be reachable with one actuator and impossible with another. The same calculation can fit one memory model and exceed another.

This does not make difficulty subjective. A locked door does not open under a better description, and a false conjecture does not become true for a weak prover. Operational difficulty concerns what can be achieved in a specified encounter: with these observations, these actions, these admitted methods, these resources, and this success condition. Its mathematical shape is a region of attainable performance, not a hidden number. The book's practical contribution is an audit: specify the encounter, calculate what is attainable, justify every translation between encounters, and establish which conclusions survive them.

Representation lives in feedback

An operative representation is whatever carries selected consequences of past interaction into the next estimate or action. It may be a posterior distribution, an observer state, a compressed transcript, or marks on paper. Its value is not its size. It is whether it preserves distinctions needed by the task. For deterministic reports, post-processing can merge indistinguishability classes but cannot split them. A finer report therefore weakly enlarges the actions that remain acceptable across every compatible state, and a quotient can make that inclusion strict.

Fix a source law for the unknown and an admitted policy whose random seed is independent of it. A representation causally built from its initial state, the full adaptive transcript, and that seed cannot contain more information about the unknown than those inputs. Processing can organize, compress, or discard evidence. It cannot invent evidence. If target accuracy requires more task-relevant information than the initial state contains, a round lower bound also needs a conditional information cap at every round. For a claim about an entire policy class, that cap must hold uniformly over every admitted policy and history.

The scalar Gaussian case makes this concrete. Repeated independent measurements add precision. Greater measurement noise means more observations are required to reach the same posterior variance. This is exact for a fixed scalar state. A dynamic Kalman filter is less simple: convergence also depends on the dynamics, process noise, observation map, and observability conditions. For continuous-time nonlinear dynamics, there is no universal Kalman observability matrix. The Hermann-Krener construction spans differentials of outputs and their iterated Lie derivatives along every admitted system vector field. Full rank is sufficient for local weak observability. With constant deficient rank and a smooth involutive annihilator, Frobenius yields local hidden leaves; a nullspace at one singular point does not. If same-input, same-output trajectories converge to each other, their hidden ambiguity is detectable. A fixed nonzero tube is practical detectability; a disturbance-gain tube that vanishes with the disturbance is a robust variant, not exact asymptotic detectability under nonzero disturbance.

Exact reconstruction is often unnecessary. If every state left indistinguishable by the admitted experiments shares one acceptable action, the agent can act robustly without knowing which state is actual. Information is task-relevant only when it changes the attainable decision.

Robustness has a margin

Nominal convergence says what an observer does inside its design model. Robustness asks what remains true when the plant, sensor, disturbance, or fault differs from that model. No robustness claim is complete until it declares the environment and model uncertainty class, its norm, and its bounds.

Suppose a one-step error inequality holds uniformly over that class, with an effective contraction factor formed from a nominal factor plus a mismatch allowance. The allowance consumes contraction margin. When the factor is below one, bounded additive disturbance gives an upper certificate: a transient plus a tube whose radius is the disturbance bound divided by the remaining margin. The tube is not an accuracy floor, nor proof that another observer cannot do better. More mismatch weakens this certificate.

If the effective factor crosses one, the contraction proof is gone. That does not prove the observer actually diverges. It means this certificate has reached its boundary. A claimed maximum degree of divergence is meaningful only inside a declared mismatch set and norm.

Fault accommodation, detection, and isolation are different. Accommodation keeps error or safety inside a bound. Detection separates faulty residuals from healthy uncertainty. Isolation separates one fault from other faults. With healthy residual radius rho, fault norm above twice rho is a sufficient deterministic detection margin at the smallest threshold certified from that radius bound alone. Candidate residual balls of radius rho are disjoint when their centers are separated by more than twice rho. These are sufficient norm bounds, not probability guarantees. A fault can be accommodated without being detected, or detected without being isolated.

High observer gain exposes the tradeoff. It can speed nominal convergence while amplifying measurement noise, making the certified robust tube larger. Fast and accurate are not the same point on the performance frontier.

Knowing does not make a path

Reachability is generated by actions. A complete map can reveal that a target is reachable and can reduce search, but it does not add a missing transformation. If a derived action only abbreviates paths already available from every state, it can shorten programs without enlarging the reachable set.

A reachable target can still require a dip in an objective. Barrier depth measures the shallowest worst dip among admissible paths. It is a bottleneck statistic, not a general cost. It does not count steps, measure energy, give search time, or determine stochastic escape probability.

Search, traversal, and possibility are therefore distinct. A path may exist but be unknown. It may be known but forbidden. It may have zero barrier and enormous length. Treating all three questions as landscape hardness hides the relevant boundary.

Behavior comes before its presentation

A differential equation, automaton, state-space model, or input/output split presents a system. In behavioral form, the system is a declared time domain, signal space, and set of admitted trajectories. Different internal models can present the same boundary behavior without being equally useful or equally faithful to the plant.

Interconnection retains trajectories compatible across shared signals, while hiding projects internal signals away. Hiding before composition can admit visible trajectories backed by incompatible hidden witnesses. If local trajectories restrict and compatible pieces have a unique admissible glue, they support a sheaf semantics on the chosen time structure. A topos can organize the resulting logic, but it does not provide the safety proof.

Safety means every admitted closed-loop trajectory remains in the safe set. For deterministic discrete dynamics this is equivalent to one-step forward invariance, but only for the behavior consisting exactly of the runs generated from the safe set. A behavior that merely contains those runs can contain unsafe ones as well; the companion checks a two-state counterexample. Empty behavior is vacuously safe, so a useful controller certificate also needs nonblocking or viability.

An abstraction is usually many-to-one. Forward simulation gives valid abstract images of concrete paths. Executing an abstract plan additionally requires every abstract step to lift from the concrete state actually reached, and abstract success to reflect concrete success. Costs, pathwise safety, and barriers need their own witnesses. A new sensor, actuator, predicate, or oracle is an extension, not a relabeling.

Difficulty has a fibred order

Asymptotic complexity claims require a family of inputs, an encoding and size measure, a machine and access model, a success criterion, and a resource measure. They concern growth across the family. An exact cost for one instance is an instance-level fact, not by itself an asymptotic theorem. A large candidate set alone is not a lower bound. One-pass input, rereadable input, and external memory define different problems.

For a fixed model, call a resource-loss pair attainable when some program stays within that resource budget and loss bound. Order pairs componentwise, with smaller resources and smaller loss better. The region is upward closed. Its nondominated attainable points, when they exist, form its Pareto front. Limiting boundary values may live only in the closure and never be attained. More time may buy accuracy. More memory may replace recomputation. Information limits or unreachable goals can send some required budgets to infinity.

Fix a comparison context: common task, instance and environment quantifiers, uncertainty class, admitted programs, interfaces, and resource-loss coordinates. Each encounter in that context has one attainable region. One encounter is at least as hard as another when its region is contained in the other's. This is a preorder on encounters, and it is typically partial: if neither region includes the other, the encounters are incomparable. Crossing frontiers make this visible, and a scalar ranking requires an extra rule for trading resources against loss.

To compare encounters whose performance coordinates differ, declare a map that translates one context's performance points into another's. Such a map is admitted only after an alignment has related the tasks, admitted worlds, interfaces, and success conditions; a unit conversion cannot repair a simulator that omits an admitted failure mode. Inverse image along the map is monotone and respects identities and composition, so the regions of all contexts assemble into an indexed family of posets: the fibred order. But the inverse image of a realized region need not be realized by any encounter in the source context. Transporting realized difficulty classes therefore needs a separate proof of closure under inverse image, and an explicit encounter transport is one sufficient witness.

The ingredients are established: Blackwell's comparison of experiments, Medvedev and Weihrauch degrees, Pareto optimization and monotone co-design, resource theories, indexed posets and fibrations. The proposal is the audit that uses them and the separation of ambient translation from transport of realized classes. No claim of priority is made.

Finite-state lower bounds illustrate the need for exact interfaces. A machine that must retain one of many distinguishable possibilities needs at least that many states. A machine with two to the power s states has s bits of capacity, not two to the power s bits. The book's positional multiplication bound is really a delayed-copy bound obtained by fixing one operand to one, not a theorem that multiplication is generally hard.

Preprocessing moves work rather than erasing it. An index, proof library, or abstraction hierarchy can be costly to build and cheap to query. A fair comparison states who receives it, whether its construction is charged, and across how many instances it is reused. Counting the cost of using a supplied lemma while omitting the search that found it measures conditional proof cost, not end-to-end discovery cost.

One robot, several encounters

A warehouse robot must leave a loading bay, choose one of two corridors, and reach a dock without collision. In one layout the left corridor is blocked; in the other, the right. A forward camera gives the same report at the fork in both layouts, so no first turn is safe for every compatible layout. The obstacle has not made motion impossible; the information boundary has made a certified turn unavailable. A side-looking lidar separates the layouts and restores a safe turn. If a fire door closes both corridors, more sensing does not help: the dock has left the reachable set, which is an action boundary rather than an information defect.

Put three controller packages in one hardware context with time, energy, memory, and clearance loss as coordinates. A slow camera planner that repositions until its view separates the layouts is safe but slow. A lidar planner is faster but uses more energy and memory. A fast camera planner buys speed at the price of a positive worst-case clearance violation. Their regions cross, so their ranking depends on a weighting policy. Whether lidar counts as a mandatory package or as an optional observation available to the same programs changes the claim, and must be stated.

Now compare simulation with hardware. The simulator records motion steps, peak planner nodes, and normalized clearance loss. Under an exact illustrative calibration, the map from hardware points to simulator points simply forgets energy. The inverse image of the simulator's region therefore contains a point with zero energy that the hardware lidar policy cannot meet, so the inverse image is strictly larger than the hardware region; this is checked in the companion. In a family of hardware encounters where every successful docking run uses some energy, no encounter realizes the translated region at all. Alignment, calibration, and realizability are three different achievements. The fibred formulation earns its use by exposing the energy constraint that the coordinate translation silently dropped.

Relational and real

Within a fixed encounter, the facts are not matters of opinion. The camera report merges the two layouts. The closed fire door removes the dock from the reachable set. A controller either meets its certified bounds or it does not. These claims are modal: a lower bound says that every admitted successful realization pays at least so much, whether or not anyone has attempted the task. Impossibility, where no admitted realization succeeds, is categorically distinct from extreme feasible difficulty, where success exists only beyond practical budgets.

The book offers a relational-dispositional interpretation: hardness is borne by the encounter, and its grounds may be distributed across the problem-object, environment, interfaces, admitted programs, and resources. That interpretation is optional. The operational results establish counterfactual constraints without settling what kind of thing hardness is. Attributing a constraint to the object's structure requires an argument that derives the bound from a structural property together with the encounter conditions. Invariance across a comparison class broadens a proved bound's scope; it does not by itself locate its ground, since every encounter in a poorly chosen class may share the same restrictive interface. Conversely, resistance that disappears after a representation change does not show that the object contributed nothing: an oracle or a new primitive can overcome a genuine obstruction for the former access model.

There is no context-free relation called "the same problem". Strict identity is equality of the specified data. Presentation equivalence is isomorphism of a declared structural reduct. Claim-relative equivalence is a pair of coherent translations that preserve and reflect a declared claim language. Directed transport preserves selected inferences in one direction only, as simulation, sound abstraction, relaxation, and reduction do. Adding an oracle, sensor, or axiom is an extension, not a change of presentation. Which relation is relevant is fixed by which structures or inferences must survive, and must be declared before the conclusion is inspected.

Objectivity beyond one context is earned by stability. A claim is stable over a declared class of licensed transformations when each of them transports it in the stated direction. Coordinate changes are a weak class; machine simulations, sensor substitutions, and environment enlargements are stronger. There is no master scale, and no result gains authority by leaving its index unstated. Formal adequacy, empirical adequacy, and normative relevance remain three separate questions, and mathematics answers only the first.

When the system reacts to the model

A model can fail because the world was unknown, because it changed, or because using the model helped change it. These call for different tests: identification, tracking, and a model of the response to the deployed policy. Reaction need not involve an opponent. A controller can heat a sensor, and a forecast can determine which observations are collected. Strategic response is the narrower case in which an agent selects behavior according to an objective, and adversarial response is narrower still.

The robust claim quantifies in a fixed order: one policy must meet its cost and loss bounds against every admitted response. Allowing a different policy after the response is known turns a failed robust task into a collection of easy tasks without improving the original solver. Holding everything else fixed, enlarging the admitted response class can only shrink the attainable region. Merely replacing one known response law by another need not shrink it.

A short calculation separates these effects. A deployed system announces a number between zero and one and pays the absolute error against the outcome. One environment always returns one. Another returns one minus the announcement. Both produce the same observation at an initial announcement of zero, so fitting that observation gives one, with zero historical error. Deployed against the reactive law, the same announcement has error one. No one deceived it; the evidence could not distinguish two response laws that agree at the data-collection policy. Each law alone admits a perfect announcement, one for the frozen law and one half for the reactive law. If one announcement must serve against both, the best worst-case loss is one third, attained at two thirds. The robust region is therefore strictly smaller than the region for either law alone. The arithmetic and both regions are machine checked. This is the setting of performative prediction, and the counterfactual comparison of policies against an adaptive environment is the concern of policy regret.

The deployment audit is short: which response laws were admitted, what did they observe, which policy generated the evidence, and does one implementable policy meet the promised bounds after deployment? A guarantee over a frozen dataset answers only part of that question. Two response rules can also agree on every interaction the learning protocol can perform while requiring different deployment policies; the first question, as in the information chapter, is whether the experiment class distinguishes the cases that matter to action.

When nearby problems stop being nearby

Nearby examples suggest how to solve a target. The suggestion becomes a transfer claim only when a proof transformation is supplied and its side conditions and costs are checked. Three costs must be kept apart: the search cost of locating a certificate, the certificate cost of constructing or checking one, and budget infeasibility, where no admitted certificate meets the bound. A search may fail while a cheap certificate exists. A failed recipe is not a lower bound.

Fix a certificate language, an admitted-proof predicate, and a cost. The proof cost of a problem is the least cost of an admitted certificate, and infinity when none exists. The extended cost gap between two costs is the larger of their truncated differences, so infinity against infinity is zero and a finite cost against infinity is infinite. The local variation of a problem is the largest gap to any neighbor within a declared radius. Separately, the available mechanisms at a budget are the kinds of admitted certificates that fit it; this set records what was available, while a selected proof label records only what someone used. A label can change while the set stays fixed, so a change of label alone locates no boundary. A sound, cost-bounded proof transformation transports available mechanisms; a relabeling does not.

Call a mechanism summary stable at a radius when every problem strictly inside that ball has an equivalent summary. The stability radius is the supremum of stable radii, and it is itself stable: any problem strictly inside the supremum ball lies inside some smaller stable ball. This needs no metric axioms. The previous edition warned that the supremum might not be attained; that warning confused the closed set of stable radii with the open ball, and the corrected theorem is machine checked.

An example shows a pivot failing while the problem stays easy. A two-equation system with a rational parameter has determinant minus one for every parameter value, so it is never singular. Substitution solves it for one subtraction. Elimination pivoting on the parameter needs six operations and is undefined when the parameter is zero. Optimal cost is therefore one everywhere, and the local variation is zero at every radius. Yet at budget six the set of available methods changes at zero, so the stability radius of that set is zero at the origin and equals the parameter's size elsewhere. A zero stability radius is compatible with constant attainable cost; it can record the loss of an optional, inefficient method rather than any increase in difficulty.

Contrast the scalar task of finding x with t times x equal to one. For nonzero t the reciprocal is an admitted certificate costing one multiplication to check. At zero, nothing is admitted and the cost is infinite, so the local variation at zero is infinite for every positive radius. Both examples contain a division that fails at zero. In the first, another admitted recipe works and the cost never moves. In the second, the equation loses every solution. Looking only at the failed division would miss exactly the distinction the framework is meant to expose.

A perturbation experiment records the admitted certificate class and budgets, tests which methods survive, and computes or bounds the attainable cost. Failed attempts are observations to explain, not universal quantifiers over all admitted proofs. Constructing, for each model, an instance that defeats it does not supply one instance defeating every model; swapping those quantifiers would invent a much stronger obstruction.

What is machine checked

The Lean companion checks the algebraic core: deterministic report refinement and decision loss, information-budget arithmetic, scalar Gaussian variance, robust and switched error recurrences, deterministic fault margins, observation-class intersections and active probing, reachability, barriers, coordinate invariance, abstraction lifting, finite-memory bounds, optima inside finite program envelopes, and the attainable-region preorders with preimage reindexing and encounter transport. For this edition it also checks decision-bearing benchmark support, extended proof costs and gaps, local variation, stability at the supremum radius, budget monotonicity and sound transport of available mechanisms, and every number in the three worked examples: the forecast losses and regions, the simulator preimage and its strict excess, the two algebraic recipes with their costs, and the singular task's infinite gap.

Verification proves derivability from the stated models. It does not establish the robot's calibration empirically, justify a certificate cost as a model of human discovery, or prove the metaphysical attribution of a ground. Those are the remaining modeling obligations, not missing algebraic steps. Six earlier formal explorations, the total category of contexts, closure operators for the three boundaries, discrete and graded Stokes laws, group completion, and a twist obstruction, survive only as collapsed source in the companion, marked peripheral, and are not used by the main argument.
